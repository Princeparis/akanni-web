# Ibadan: A City Made and Remade

The owner’s supplied research brief is the foundation for this revision: [original brief](research/ibadan-history-brief.md). Its creative proposals guide the experience; source consultation limits remain intact. This implementation preserves the existing Next.js, Three.js, GSAP, Lenis and Web Audio architecture and the ten playground projects.

## Experience treatment

The narrative asks: **Who gets to build a city, and whose work does the city remember?** Six acts follow eighteen episodes from earlier settlement traditions to an open present. Political achievement is considered alongside displacement, gender, enslaved labour, resistance, ordinary work and artistic exchange. The model separates historical claims, their evidence status, named historical people, composite viewpoints, locations, interpretive staging and unresolved questions.

A centred invitation on the portfolio homepage opens a brief, reduced-motion-aware transition into `/ibadan`. Its centred entry looks into an interpretive landscape with a carried object and composite household. Enter starts a restrained ground-level camera movement and optional atmosphere, and requests browser fullscreen from the same gesture. The experience remains full viewport if fullscreen is unsupported or denied. The text edition can be entered directly.

The main scene is a spatial environment, with people, courtyards, objects and paths. Its point treatment is retained, while movement follows explicit viewpoints. There is no continuous camera orbit or random object explosion. Episode changes crossfade environments; the camera reframes over 2.4 seconds. “The story” establishes the historical anchor; viewpoint controls reveal people, institutions and consequences. Separate people and evidence panels keep named actors and interpretation close to the scene. A six-act menu expands dated episodes and previews the hovered/focused destination.

## Evidence and provenance

- `src/data/ibadan.ts`: six acts and eighteen complete episode records. `historicalClaims` carry source IDs and evidence status; `people` distinguish historical subjects and composite viewpoints. Unverified coordinates remain null. Dates are not invented to fill chronology gaps; Efunsetan’s decade-spanning episode has no artificial precise sort year.
- `src/data/ibadan-sources.ts`: all S01–S23 records transcribed from the supplied brief, including whether the author consulted full text, an abstract or an indexed excerpt, and any failed access/incomplete metadata.
- No independent archive visit, physical survey, full-text retrieval of every source, authenticated historical portrait or source quotation is claimed by this implementation.
- Conventional dates and oral traditions remain visibly distinct. The 1886 settlement and the winding down by 1893 are separate. The 1893 correspondence’s domestic-slavery assurances remain visible. WNTV uses 31 October 1959 without an unqualified continental-priority claim. Cocoa House completion (1964) and commissioning (1965) are separate.
- Mapo’s schematic façade appears only in its 1929 episode. Modern Bodija appears only in the later state-capital episode. Regional events explicitly identify locations outside Ibadan and use schematic relationships, not invented coordinates.

See [visual assets and unresolved evidence](ibadan-asset-plan.md) for the boundaries of the reconstruction.

## Sound and narration

Atmosphere is original Web Audio synthesis; no archival authenticity is implied. It starts after the visitor’s gesture, can be muted independently, retunes between episodes, suspends when hidden and closes on leaving. Narration ducks the atmosphere automatically. Captions and the complete transcript remain readable.

A free `edge-tts` client was installed in a separate workspace tool environment for feasibility testing. Its Microsoft speech service returned a proxy HTTP 403; no generated recordings were obtained or shipped. That optional tool is **not** an application dependency.

The working optional narrator uses the browser’s Speech Synthesis API and the episode’s editorial script. It prefers an installed `en-NG` voice, then available African English locales, then other English voices. A selector shows the actual available voices. It does not label another accent as Nigerian, clone a named person, or claim the output is archival. Availability, voice quality and pronunciation depend on the visitor’s device. Playback errors preserve the journey and transcript; narration cancels on leaving or hiding the page. A mocked available Nigerian voice is used in the browser test, not claimed as an installed voice in headless Chromium.

## Native scrolling and menus

The [Lenis nested-scroll documentation](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis#nested-scroll) was read online and checked against the installed 1.3.11 implementation. `data-lenis-prevent` is checked before Lenis’s stopped-state handler. The global dialog and `.menu-sheet` now carry it, alongside native overflow, `pan-y` and overscroll containment. The background remains locked, while wheel and touch scrolling reach the final navigation link and email. The close row stays sticky. A focus/hover preview and scroll-progress indicator make destination selection visible.

The journey menu, source panels and text edition also have explicit native scroll containers. Reduced motion removes animated entrance effects and camera travel. Rendering stops for the text edition and hidden tabs. Audio, narration, observers, listeners, geometry and animation frames are disposed on exit. WebGL failure opens a complete text edition and offers a retry.
