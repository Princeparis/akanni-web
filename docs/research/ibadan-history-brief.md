# Ibadan: A City Made and Remade
## Historical research and 3D experience brief for Codex

Prepared 24 October 2026. Research cutoff: this date.
Deliverable: a sourced historical foundation and a creative production brief, not an authenticated architectural reconstruction.
Recommended format: a spatial, chapter-based experience with a chronological spine and optional people/place explorations.

## 1. The central idea

Explore how people made Ibadan: settlers seeking security, commanders accumulating power, women organizing commerce, enslaved people sustaining households and production, religious communities building institutions, and later students, artists and broadcasters reshaping public life.

The visitor should leave understanding both achievement and its costs. Ibadan's history cannot be adequately told through landmark opening dates or a succession of heroic rulers.

Working title: **Ibadan: A City Made and Remade**.
Narrative question: **Who gets to build a city, and whose work does the city remember?**

These are original editorial proposals, not historical quotations.

## 2. Evidence rules

Every historical claim should carry a source ID from section 8 and one of these labels:

- **Documented:** supported by the consulted institutional, scholarly or historical source. This does not mean every surrounding scene detail is known.
- **Conventional date:** a commonly used date for a gradual process, rather than a precisely documented beginning.
- **Oral tradition / disputed chronology:** culturally significant testimony whose dating or details vary.
- **Creative reconstruction:** an invented staging, composite person, camera movement, soundscape or dialogue.
- **Needs verification:** insufficient evidence for a detailed factual depiction.

Sources were consulted through online pages, PDFs and indexed excerpts. Access limits are recorded below. No archive visit, oral-history interview, image licensing or physical site survey was performed. This is sufficient to guide the project's historical structure; it is not clearance to invent exact costumes, building plans or spoken quotations.

### Crucial corrections

1. Do not collapse Lagelu's earlier settlement traditions into a simple claim that he founded the modern military settlement in 1829. Distinguish earlier Ibadan traditions from the nineteenth-century city's formation. [S01–S03]
2. Do not call every nineteenth-century head an “Olubadan” without explaining period titles. Preserve historically sourced titles such as Baale, Bashorun, Balogun and Aare.
3. Efunsetan Aniwura's dramatic and cinematic portrayals are not transparent records of her life. Scholarship explicitly examines how they shaped and distorted her public image. [S06]
4. Separate the 1886 peace settlement from the broader war's winding down by 1893. Do not depict one agreement as instantly ending all regional hostilities. [S07]
5. The 1893 colonial agreement was not a simple transaction in which Britain immediately abolished slavery. The correspondence reproduced by Samuel Johnson includes British assurances concerning the continuation of domestic slavery. [S08]
6. Say WNTV launched in Ibadan on 31 October 1959. Avoid an unqualified “Africa's first television station”; that slogan needs qualification because earlier North African broadcasting existed. [S13, S14]
7. Cocoa House's completion and commissioning are different milestones: its owner describes completion in 1964 and commissioning in 1965. [S16]
8. Today's rusted metal-roof skyline is not an automatic reference for an 1829 scene. Every period needs its own visual evidence.

## 5. The experience structure

### Recommended main path

Use six acts, with the timeline entries as expandable chapters:

1. **Finding a place:** founding traditions, migration and the war camp.
2. **Building power:** commanders, compounds, military success and expansion.
3. **Living under power:** trade, gender, slavery, religion and resistance.
4. **Changing the rules:** colonial negotiation, rail and civic administration.
5. **Making a modern public:** university, television, arts and political crisis.
6. **The unfinished city:** work, water, memory and present-day choices.

The emotional progression should be curiosity, attachment, complication, consequence and reflection. This is a proposed editorial structure.

### A concrete opening, not a generic fly-through

Start close to the ground with a carried household object. The visitor sees terrain before seeing a skyline. A restrained label explains that the opening household is a creative reconstruction. As the visitor arrives, a timeline separates “earlier settlement traditions” from “c.1829: modern military settlement.”

From there, give three paths: follow a household, examine the settlement, or inspect the evidence. This immediately establishes place, people and historical uncertainty.

### Spatial anchors

- **Mapo and the historic core:** relationships among authority, compounds, streets and commerce.
- **Dugbe / Ogunpa / Old Gbagi:** transport and changing trade networks.
- **University settings:** knowledge production and new social possibilities.
- **WNTV and Mbari:** distinct spaces for broadcasting and artistic exchange.
- **Waterways and terrain:** a continuing environmental thread across periods.
- **Regional map:** Osogbo, Ijaiye and Kiriji belong to Ibadan's story but are not all locations within Ibadan.

These are navigation anchors, not surveyed routes. Do not invent coordinates.

### Interactions that carry meaning

- Inspect a document to reveal its source, date, perspective and omissions.
- Follow a commodity to see the people and institutions involved.
- Change viewpoint to understand the same event from another position.
- Compare a location across periods while labelling reconstructed geometry.
- Reveal the difference between historical evidence and later representation.
- Explore a relationship map showing dependence, alliance and conflict.

Avoid interactions that simply make objects spin without adding understanding.

## 6. Visual, material and sound direction

This is an art-direction proposal pending reference validation.

Use topography, density, courtyards, changes in roofscape and the relationship between public routes and private compounds to distinguish Ibadan. Avoid a generic “African village” kit.

Build an era-specific reference board for each chapter:

| Asset class | Required evidence before detailed production |
|---|---|
| Building shape and layout | Dated plans, photographs, written descriptions, or clearly stated comparative reconstruction. |
| Roof and wall materials | Evidence appropriate to the date and social setting. No universal rusty-roof filter. |
| Dress, hair and adornment | Date, occupation, community and status; do not give every resident elite ceremonial clothing. |
| Weapons and equipment | Verified regional use and period. Avoid fantasy armour and casually mixed firearms. |
| Railway and broadcast hardware | Dated institutional photographs, catalogues or archive references. |
| Market goods, measures and money | Period-specific trade evidence. Modern packaging and currency must not appear early. |
| Religious objects and practices | Community-informed references and correct setting; avoid decorative misuse. |
| Portraits | Authenticated likeness where available; otherwise an explicitly interpretive visual. |
| Sound | Licensed or original recordings; language review; no claim of archival authenticity for generated audio. |

Let sound change with the era: footsteps, work, negotiation, transport, school, broadcasting and performance. These are proposed layers, not recordings recovered from history.

Include Islam, Christianity and indigenous Yoruba religious life as parts of the city's lived history. The Islamic-education study supplies an evidence base for schools and community organisation under colonial rule. [S23] Obtain specific local religious histories before staging named rites or dating a mosque.

Use restrained camera motion, optional captions, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion settings and a readable non-3D mode. The evidence should remain available even if the visitor cannot run the 3D scene.

## 7. Verification priorities before final historical reconstruction

These are scoped production gates, not a request to postpone the whole project.

1. **Origins:** compare Akinyele, Bolanle Awe and local accounts. Do not merge contradictory dates into a fabricated consensus.
2. **Early settlement:** verify period compound forms and street relationships. Current buildings are not proof of earlier geometry.
3. **Biographies:** confirm titles and dates from full works; online lists disagree, including about Ogunmola's chronology.
4. **Efunsetan:** consult Bolanle Awe's biography alongside Adeboye's critique; separate documentary claims from theatrical characters.
5. **Kiriji:** consult academic histories and treaty material; verify camps, dates and equipment before detailed military scenes.
6. **Mapo:** obtain original construction/commissioning documentation and dated images.
7. **Political crisis:** obtain full scholarship, contemporary newspapers and multiple perspectives before writing a specific street incident.
8. **Religious history:** verify Muslim, Christian and indigenous institutions with local custodians and relevant scholarship.
9. **Ordinary lives:** pursue market histories, oral histories and family archives to replace composites where feasible.
10. **Present-day assets:** photograph or survey relevant places with permission; confirm rights and dates.

Candidate research routes include the National Archives in Ibadan, University of Ibadan collections, the National Library repository, relevant family/compound custodians, religious communities, railway collections and broadcast archives. Their current access arrangements have not been checked.

Questions for oral-history work:
- What part of this place's history did you learn from an eyewitness, and what was passed down?
- Which building or street features have changed?
- Which account does your family or community dispute?
- Whose work or experience is usually left out?
- May your words, name, voice and images be used, and under what conditions?

Record consent and provenance separately from interpretation.
