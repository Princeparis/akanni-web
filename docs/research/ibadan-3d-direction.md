Its proposals are reference material. The owner subsequently chose to retain the original particle medium and use the improved detailed models as its source.

## Art direction

The experience is a particle history with an editorial reading layer: one large chronological marker, one main subject, one reading column, and a persistent eighteen-stop timeline. On phones the subject sits above the reading area. Short screens preserve native scrolling within the story.

The entry and homepage share the same portrait study, centred wordmark and warm ink/champagne particle palette. The homepage's expanding transition opens directly into the story, without a second entrance click. Direct visits retain the explicit atmosphere and native fullscreen gesture. A homepage transition starts quietly; audio and browser fullscreen remain available in its header.

The main renderer uses detailed triangle surfaces as sampling geometry for point clouds, with extra facial density, per-particle normals and depth occlusion. Visible colour comes entirely from point sprites. Visitors can turn objects by dragging or inspect them with keyboard-accessible rotate/reset controls. Chapter changes move the old object out and introduce the next in one direction; camera motion is restrained and ends. There is no perpetual orbit. Rendering is on demand, including while text is being read, and pauses when hidden. Reduced motion uses immediate compositions.

## Historical and asset limits

The Oluyole and Efunsetan portraits are **artist's interpretations**, not authenticated likenesses. The modern artist is a composite, not a claimed portrait of Soyinka. Their face topology is adapted graphical data, not an archive scan; clothing and accessories are original interpretive artwork, not verified costume evidence. The same distinction appears in object inspection and source panels.

The objects and architectural models are original illustrated studies. Mapo Hall and Cocoa House use recognisable architectural features but are not measured reconstructions. The locomotive and television are generic period-inspired forms, not identified equipment from the railway or WNTV. Blank paper is used instead of fabricated historical writing or signatures. The vessel/water composition is not a calibrated flood map.

The research brief's source register and access limits are preserved. This pass independently verifies model provenance and rendering references, not every primary historical source. Exact personal likenesses, costume authentication and surveyed architecture would require additional appropriate visual evidence.

## Provenance and rebuilding

`ibadan-model-sources.json` records every graphical input URL, byte size and SHA256 digest. `scripts/build-ibadan-models.py` verifies those digests before building. To reproduce, download the listed files into one directory (using their final filenames) and pass that directory to the script. Outputs are `public/ibadan/models/iyalode-study.glb` and `commander-study.glb`. The public asset directory retains the full upstream CC0 licence. The original runtime object/clothing models are in `src/components/ibadan/gallery-models.ts`.

The fourteen lightweight WebP previews are renders of those same models. With a local Next server running, `IBADAN_PREVIEW_ORIGIN=http://127.0.0.1:3000 node scripts/render-ibadan-posters.mjs` regenerates them using Playwright. Set `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE_PATH` when using a system Chromium. The script removes its temporary public render harness when it finishes. No archive photographs, protected artworks or unidentified collection downloads are shipped. Models and previews are served from the app itself. The sampler and shader are shared by the live experience and preview renderer.
